The rice

A slender, aromatic long grain

Hashemi is a slender, aromatic long-grain rice grown on the Caspian plain of Mazandaran. It is harvested once a year.

After harvest the rice is aged. Its grains cook long, fluffy and separate.

Each carton holds 5 kg (11 lb) in a vacuum-sealed inner bag. An arched window in the front panel lets the buyer see the grain before the carton is opened.

Each can holds 2 kg (4.4 lb) in a vacuum-sealed inner bag. The telescoping lid lifts straight off, and a gold paper strip across lid and body shows the can has not been opened.

Origin

From the paddies of Fereydunkenar

Fereydunkenar, Mazandaran, Iran

Binj Hashemi is grown in the paddies of Fereydunkenar, on the Caspian plain of Mazandaran in northern Iran.

The rice is harvested once a year and aged before it is packed. The name of its origin is printed on the front of every carton and can: Fereydunkenar · Mazandaran · Iran.

Cooking

Three steps to separate, fragrant grains

  1. 1

    Rinse and soak

    Rinse 3–4 times, then soak for 2 hours in salted water.

  2. 2

    Boil

    Boil 8–10 minutes until just tender at the core. Drain.

  3. 3

    Steam

    Return to the pot with oil or butter. Steam covered on low heat for 45 minutes.

Storage

Keep sealed in a cool, dry place. Once opened, store in an airtight container.
